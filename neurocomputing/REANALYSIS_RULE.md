## 0. Status and scope

### 0.1 What this rule is

It fixes, before any new number is computed, the interval and test methods, the multiplicity control, the designation of primary and exploratory results, the exact estimands, the decision categories and their wording, and the title decision for the Neurocomputing re-analysis.

### 0.2 What "primary" means here

P1 to P4 re-test estimates whose point values and percentile intervals were known when this rule was written (Section 1.1), with a method and a multiplicity control fixed in advance. They are not tests of unseen outcomes. Only P5 (whole-display rotation and its orientation-matched controls, new displays) and P6 (new readouts on the registers checkpoint and on DINOv2-B blocks 7 and 11) test outcomes that nobody had seen; related values that shape expectations for P6 were known and are listed in Section 1.1, item 17. The paper says this in its Methods.

### 0.3 What this rule guards against

It guards against choosing among interval methods, among rotation measures and among wordings after seeing their results. The percentile intervals of every P1 to P4 estimate were known when it was written, the interval method was chosen with them in view, and the two rotation measures of P4 (U20 and A20) were chosen with their values in view. The null family, the alternating displays and readout R1n were introduced after the version-2 results had been seen (Section 1.1, item 16). It does not guard against knowledge of the data.

### 0.4 Author decisions applied (18 November 2026)

- D4: this rule and its code are pushed publicly before any re-scoring.
- D5: the readout-free token analysis is reported under both nulls; the null pre-specified on 15 October 2026 is the analysis of record; no headline dissociation.
- D6: two GPU runs after the current GPU job ends, never before: G3 (readouts R1n, R1, R2, R3 on `facebook/dinov2-with-registers-base`, blocks 6 and 12, seeds 0, 1, 2) and A14 (R1n, R1, R2, R3, R4, seeds 0, 1, 2, on public DINOv2-B hidden states 7 and 11 concatenated, and on hidden state 11 alone in the same forward pass). The stronger title stays only if every readout meeting the accuracy criterion on the registers checkpoint and on hidden states 7 and 11 keeps R4's sign with an interval excluding zero; otherwise the scoped title of Critic fix 1. The analysis and the title decision, with the precisions added after the reviews, are fixed in Section 5.6.
- D7: the whole-display rigid rotation control runs on the CPU after this rule is public; it is primary contrast P5; if property 1 shrinks, the paper leads with the alternation result.
- Defaults: xSoyelNet-ASPP goes to the supplement; xSoyelNet-ML appears only for the same-tensor case; configural claims are worded "non-local alignment sensitivity" (no contour-completion null is built); TranSalNet is not added (no weight downloads).

13. Properties of the methods on synthetic data (`tests_output.txt`, run before any real score was analysed with them; and an independent check by the statistics reviewer, `reviews/support/independent_check_output.txt`): with 12 clusters and symmetric errors the wild cluster percentile-t interval covered close to 0.95 (0.956 in the test file, 0.958 in the independent check; two-stage percentile interval 0.92 to 0.93); with 6 clusters and full enumeration 0.945 and 0.960 (percentile 0.85). Under strongly skewed cluster effects no interval method tested held nominal coverage with 6 or 12 clusters (coverage 0.83 to 0.90 in two independent stress cases; WCR size 0.09 to 0.16), because symmetric weights cannot correct skewness; the wild cluster intervals were never worse than the two-stage percentile intervals. The pairs cluster BCa interval for a ratio of means covered 0.913 (Fieller 0.927), so P4 counts may be slightly liberal. With 6 clusters the smallest attainable two-sided p is 6 / 46,656 = 0.00013, but for a strong effect with unequal cluster sums p is about the share of sign-constant weight vectors, in our simulations at most 2 x 3^6 / 6^6 = 0.031 (observed 0.0096 for t = 7.5; largest in 361 random datasets with t above 5: 0.0123); it approaches the lower bound only when the six cluster sums are nearly equal. ## 2. Data, units and conventions

1. Configurations: 60 Kanizsa configurations (square side 190 px, triangle side 230 px; support ratio 0.3, 0.5, 0.7; dark and light; 5 positions). Clusters: the 12 shape x support ratio x polarity cells, 6 per shape, positions within clusters, ordered as `v2/scores/analyze_v3.design` (sorted by the string of the key). The design has 12 clusters (6 per shape); `nn/registers/REGISTERS_RESULTS.md` says "20 ... cells" in its Design line, a typo that is not copied anywhere.
2. Measure: r = M(inside) / (M(inside) + M(inducer discs)), from the q16 map (`r_inside`), the measure of every rotation analysis. For the swap cells it equals the v2 "inside" measure of Table 3 to the printed precision (checked by the sanity gate). Unit of analysis: one value per configuration; readouts: seeds averaged per configuration, then the 336 and 448 px values averaged.
3. Effects and losses: E(test) = r(test) - r(control180); L(theta) = r(illusory) - r(mis theta) (figure-mass loss, control-free); square Delta(theta) = r(alt theta) - r(mis theta), alt = mean of phases a and b; U(theta) = E(mis theta) / E(illusory); A(theta) = E(alt theta) / E(illusory); ratios are ratios of means.

Models: DeepGaze IIE and DeepGaze MSDB ("DeepGaze MSDB" is the public repository's name for the model of Kummerer, Khanuja and Bethge 2025; the Methods say so once); the 15 local nulls (corner, ink, Sobel edge energy, Gaussian sigma 8, 16, 25, 40, 60 px); the 60 swap heads (DINOv2-B blocks 12 and 6 of `facebook/dinov2-base`, SigLIP-L/16 block 24 of `google/siglip-large-patch16-256`, the ShapeNet-C ResNet-50 of DeepGaze IIE; R1n, R1, R2, R3, R4; seeds 0, 1, 2); the 18 sweep readouts (single seed); the 6 registers R4 heads; after D6, the G3 and A14 heads.
5. Block numbering: block L = Hugging Face `hidden_states[L]` (output of block L, 1-based, before the final layer norm). ## 4. Interval and test methods

1. Linear contrasts (any mean of per-configuration values d_i: an effect, a loss, a model-minus-null difference): wild cluster bootstrap with Webb six-point weights (+/- sqrt(1/2), +/- 1, +/- sqrt(3/2), each with probability 1/6; Cameron, Gelbach and Miller 2008; Webb 2023).
   - Standard error: CR1 cluster-robust, se = sqrt(G / (G - 1) x sum over clusters of (sum of residuals in the cluster)^2) / N.
   - Test (WCR): residuals with the null imposed (d_i - mu0, mu0 = 0 unless stated), t* = (mean* - mu0) / se*; two-sided equal-tailed p. With 12 clusters, 9,999 random weight vectors from numpy RandomState(2026) and p = 2 (1 + min(#{t* >= t}, #{t* <= t})) / (9,999 + 1); with 6 clusters (and 4 for exploratory item 18), all 6^G weight vectors are enumerated and p = 2 min(share of t* >= t, share of t* <= t). A directional claim needs p < .05 and the point estimate in the stated direction.
   - Interval (WCU): unrestricted residuals, percentile-t interval [mean - q(0.975) se, mean - q(0.025) se].
   - Decisions (P1 to P3, P5, P6 and the title rule) use the WCR p-value. The WCU interval is the reported interval estimate. When the WCR decision at .05 and the WCU interval disagree on whether zero is excluded, both are reported and the WCR p decides. Condition (d) of Section 5.6 is the one place where the WCU interval decides (an interval of the opposite sign).
   - With 6 clusters the smallest attainable p is 0.00013 and, for strong effects with unequal clusters, p is typically 0.005 to 0.031 (Section 1.1, item 13). This is a property of the method, stated before any result.
   - Diagnostic, never decision-bearing: for every primary linear contrast the sample skewness of its cluster means (`scipy.stats.skew`) is reported next to the result, because the intersection-union claims of P1, P2 and P5 take each component at a nominal .05 and are liberal under strong skew (Section 1.1, item 13).
2. Ratios and ratio differences: pairs cluster bootstrap (the two-stage v2 resample, 10,000 resamples, RandomState(2026)) with the BCa adjustment (Efron 1987): bias correction from the share of resampled values below the estimate, acceleration from the delete-one-cluster jackknife. p-values by inverting the BCa interval (the level at which the tested value is an endpoint). A Fieller interval (CR1 covariance, t with G - 1 degrees of freedom) is reported next to every single ratio, including both ratios of each P4 contrast, as a check; when BCa and Fieller disagree on whether a value is excluded from a single ratio's interval, both are reported and BCa decides. A Fieller interval does not exist for a difference of ratios, so this rule does not apply to the P4 estimand itself.
3. Stability: a ratio is "unstable" if the |t| of its denominator (mean / CR1 se) is below 4. An unstable ratio is reported with the flag, never counted, and never used in the title, abstract, highlights or contributions. A ratio is reported only when the 95% WCU interval of its denominator excludes 0 (otherwise "not reportable"), and always next to the difference it summarises. Losses are always reported as differences.
4. Multiplicity: Holm (1979; cited by its JSTOR stable URL, no DOI) within the family stated for each contrast; the intersection-union test (Berger and Hsu 1996) where a claim requires every component, each component at .05 with no correction. No correction across families; each family has alpha = .05. Exploratory results are unadjusted. Holm is used for the counts of P3 and P6; for the claim that every valid cell keeps the sign it is conservative, since that claim is an intersection that the intersection-union test already controls. P6 families are per feature set because each new feature set is a separate claim; the title condition combines them as an intersection.
5. Continuity: the published two-stage percentile intervals are reported in the supplement for every primary estimate.
6. Implementation: `analyze_re.py` (numpy and scipy only), tested in `test_analyze_re.py` on synthetic data with known truth (coverage, exact enumeration, BCa against scipy, BCa inversion, Holm, Fieller, stability, the P1 to P6 logic, the P5 estimand under an orientation response of the control, the P5 categories and the abstract rule, the title decision with every condition of Section 5.6, the published rounding of the accuracy criterion); the test output is `tests_output.txt`. The feature-path check of the new feature sets is `test_features_cpu.py` with output `tests_features_output.txt`.

### 5.5 P5: whole-display rigid rotation with orientation-matched controls (mock review A9; decision D7; redefined after the design review, finding 1)

- Stimuli: `stim_rigid/` (`render_rigid.py`; md5 sums in `stim_rigid/manifest.json`).
  - Rigid rotations: for each of the 60 configurations and phi = 10, 20, 30, 45 degrees, the stim_v3 illusory display and its 180 degree control, each rotated rigidly by phi about the figure centre (the vertex centroid), clockwise on screen (the sign convention of the stim_v3 uniform rotations); inducer centres and notch directions rotate together, so the illusory figure is kept and its edges become oblique. 480 displays, none refused for leaving the canvas. Same canvas, background, colours, supersampling and antialiasing as stim_v3; at phi = 0 the code reproduces the stim_v3 PNG files pixel for pixel and their AOI files exactly. Each rigid display is scored with its own rotated AOIs (inside, inducer discs, core). For every phi, the notch edges of the rigid display have exactly the orientations of the stim_v3 uniform rotation by phi; only the inducer centres differ.
  - Orientation-matched controls of the uniform rotation: for each configuration and theta = 10, 20, 30, the display mis<theta>_control180, in which every control180 inducer is turned by a further +theta about its own centre at the unrotated positions (rotation 180 + theta; stim_v3 mis<theta> has rotation theta). Its notch edges have the orientations of stim_v3 mis<theta>, with outward notches. 180 displays, scored with the stim_v3 AOIs of their configuration. Checks recorded in the manifest: the same code with rotation theta reproduces the stim_v3 mis<theta> PNG pixel for pixel (180 of 180); notch directions equal those of mis<theta> plus 180 exactly; the gradient-orientation histogram correlates at least 0.9915 with mis<theta> and at most 0.885 with the unrotated control180; ink mass within 0.17% of control180.
- Why the controls: with I = r(illusory), C = r(control180), M = r(mis theta), RI = r(rig theta illusory), RC = r(rig theta control180) and MC = r(mis theta control180), write each score as a baseline plus o (the change produced by turning every notch edge by theta; o_in for inward, o_out for outward notches), a (the alignment effect lost when inward inducers are misaligned) and g (the change produced by rotating the configuration on the canvas; g_in, g_out). The drafted estimand (RI - M) - (RC - C) equals a + (g_in - g_out) - o_out: it keeps the orientation response of the control, because C has cardinal edges while RC has oblique ones. Every term of P5 is therefore a contrast against a control with the same edge orientation.
- Models: decision-bearing: DeepGaze IIE and DeepGaze MSDB against the 15 local nulls. Exploratory: the 60 swap heads, the 18 sweep readouts, the 6 registers heads (and the G3 and A14 heads if they are scored on these displays later). M, I and C come from the published stim_v3 scores; RI, RC and MC from the new CPU scoring, whose pipeline check (Section 3.2) reproduces the published values to 1e-4.
- Per configuration i and theta (primary theta = 20):
  - E(0) = I - C; E_rig(theta) = RI - RC; E'_mis(theta) = M - MC;
  - L_uni(theta) = E(0) - E'_mis(theta): the loss under uniform rotation, in the contrast currency;
  - L_rig(theta) = E(0) - E_rig(theta): the loss produced by turning edges and figure together, alignment kept;
  - A*(theta) = L_uni(theta) - L_rig(theta) = E_rig(theta) - E'_mis(theta) = a + (g_in - g_out): the alignment effect at matched edge orientation; it compares an aligned and a misaligned display whose notch edges have the same orientation, each against a control with the same edge orientation;
  - C*(theta) = L_uni(theta) - 2 L_rig(theta) = A*(theta) - L_rig(theta): positive when rigid rotation reproduces less than half of the uniform-rotation loss;
  - rigid share s*(theta) = L_rig(theta) / L_uni(theta) (BCa, stability flag and denominator check on L_uni, Fieller alongside).
- Tests at theta = 20, per model M:
  - (i) alignment effect beyond every null: A*_M,i(20) - A*_k,i(20) > 0 for all 15 nulls (WCR, 12 clusters, direction positive; intersection-union test);
  - (ii) half criterion: C*_M(20) > 0 (WCR, direction positive).
  - Power, stated before any result: (ii) has modest power. From the published percentile half-widths, if rigid rotation removed none of the loss, the expected t of C* is roughly 1.4 to 2.0 for MSDB and 1.8 to 2.6 for IIE (power about 0.3 to 0.7, depending on the correlation of E and E_rig across configurations), and lower still because L_uni is itself a difference of two contrasts. A model can therefore land in category 2b even when rigid rotation removes none of the loss; category 2b wording says only that the half criterion was undecided.
- Outcome categories (per model; fixed wording in Section 7.2):
  - Category 1, "survives": (i) and (ii) met.
  - Category 2a, "shrinks": (i) met, (ii) not met, L_uni > 0 and s* >= 0.5 (point estimate).
  - Category 2b, "half criterion undecided": (i) met, (ii) not met, otherwise (s* < 0.5, or L_uni not positive).

- The receptive-field result is quoted with the verbatim I-d wording of Section 0.5 and its companions (single seed; intervals reflect stimulus sampling only; ERF and depth collinear within each network, rho = +0.76; network identity confounds objective with data, size and patch size; "no evidence for a peak at an intermediate receptive field"; the narrow ERF50 range 238.5 to 325.5 px, a factor of 1.36, for which a plausible but untested reason is that every sweep readout shares the ASPP design; negative point estimates within all three networks; the stimulus CI excludes zero because it holds the receptive fields fixed and resamples only the displays, while the rule's test of association is the permutation test).

## 8. Dependencies and order

1. No model or null is scored on any stimulus for this re-analysis, and no statistic of this rule is computed on any real score, before all of the following hold: (a) the files of Section 10 are committed and pushed to the public repository `Deep-c-Vision-LTD/looking-at-nothing`; (b) `REANALYSIS_RULE.sha256` records their SHA-256 and the UTC time of fixing; (c) `PUBLIC_PUSH.txt` records the public commit hash and its URL. This is enforced in code: `analyze_re.py` (every step, the sanity gate included), `scoring/score_rigid.py`, `scoring/local_nulls_rigid.py` and `gpu/eval_re.py`, `gpu/train_re.py`, `gpu/stats_re.py`, `gpu/analyze_gpu.py` call `require_public()` before anything else, which refuses unless both files exist, every file hashed in `REANALYSIS_RULE.sha256` is unchanged and every stimulus file matches its md5 in `stim_rigid/manifest.json`; `gpu/run_gpu_re.sh` runs the same check before its queue. Every script written later for this re-analysis (for example the exploratory scripts of `RUN_PLAN.md`) calls it too. The sanity gate (Section 3) is the first step after the push.
2. GPU runs (G3, A14) start only after the GPU job that held the GPU on 18 November 2026 has ended: the ablation runner (`ablation/run_all.sh`) has exited and its log ends with its analyze step, no other GPU job runs, and the author has created `gpu/GO_AHEAD`. `gpu/run_gpu_re.sh` refuses to start while an ablation runner is alive and, before every step, waits until `gpu/gpu_idle.sh` reports no heavy job twice, 60 s apart; a Python process holding a Metal client counts as heavy whatever its CPU share. No MPS or Metal call is made by any CPU step while that job runs.
3. CPU steps while the GPU job runs: one Python process at a time, `nice -n 10`, `OMP_NUM_THREADS=4`, `HF_HUB_OFFLINE=1`, `TRANSFORMERS_OFFLINE=1`; any script that imports `ab_objective` uses the stub in `_stub/` (no backend query). MSDB scoring of `stim_rigid` runs as one process (overnight while the GPU job runs, or after it), never in shards while G3 or A14 run.
4. Order after the push: sanity gate; P1 to P4; exploratory analyses from saved outputs; rigid-rotation scoring and P5 (with P1 and P2 for the abstract rule); GPU runs and P6; the title decision last. A result of one family never changes the definition of another.

## 9. Deviations

- Every departure from this file is logged in `REANALYSIS_DEVIATIONS.md` (published with the results) with the UTC date, what changed, why, whether any result of the affected family had been seen, and its effect; the result under the rule as written is reported next to the result under the deviation.
- Not allowed after any result of the family has been seen: changing a family's membership (including the P4 list and the P6 validity rule), a threshold (alpha, |t| = 4, the 0.03 margin, the half criterion, the minimum of two valid readouts), an estimand, a category definition, the title rule, or retraining a G3 or A14 cell (Section 5.6).
- Code errors found later are fixed, logged and reported with both values. Because the guard of Section 8.1 checks the hashes, a fixed file can only be changed together with a logged deviation and a new public record of the changed file.
- A run that cannot be completed (for example, the GPU window closes) is reported as not run; the title rule treats it as failing.
- If a required input turns out to be missing or corrupted, the affected contrast is reported as not computable, with the reason; no substitute input is used without a logged deviation.
